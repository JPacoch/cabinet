*For friends who are still looking*

## The thing,

Ever had that feeling when everything seems almost too much - not because of any singular crisis but because of the sheer, relentless weight of it? The work is done, yet a quiet unease lingers that nothing’s actually finished - or worse - that it will never really end. You come back from your third vacation of the year still unfulfilled, with neither the time nor the money for a fourth. Your father dies and grief doesn’t even give you room to breathe - it dumps a pile of logistics at your feet: consoling your wrecked sister, organizing the funeral, negotiating days off at work.

Beneath the noise there is something else. A quiet fracture where the routine suddenly stops making sense. It never lets you truly catch the next big thing, never lets you rest. No matter how skillfully you plan, the moment you stop fooling yourself, it is still there -breathing warm, uncomforting air onto the back of your shoulder. There are many names for it.[^1]

## haunting you,

We have invented countless ways to work around it. Most of us try to outrun that tremulous feeling by turning toward something brighter, something that promises coherence or relief. A new hobby, state-of-the-art psychotherapy, money, successful career, social media, alcohol, religion. Some of these are healthy, some are destructive - none of seem inherently evil. Yet the moment we ask any of them to *solve* that underlying dread, they all become the same thing: an escape.

And I wouldn’t necessarily blame anyone for taking it, not myself either.[^2] After all, seventy-something years of raw unfiltered dread doesn’t look like a livable life. None of us wakes up thinking that that is what we deserve.

## it is called

Camus spent pages circling around that exact phenomena in *The Myth of Sisyphus*. What he saw wasn’t simply that the world is cruel or that human beings are weak; unfulfilled and sorrowful almost like an anti-nietzschean hero. The wound isn’t in us (nor in others) and it isn’t out there in the cold indifference of the universe. It lives strictly in the collision between the two - our stubborn inherent hunger for clarity and purpose crashing directly into what he called the "unreasonable silence of the world." We ask why and nothing, literally nothing answers.  Once facade collapses and you hear that silence clearly, you realize what you’ve been running from all along -

## the Absurd

Maybe there is no way around it; maybe all there is to do is keep going. When the facts of our lives refuse to bend to our need for logic the only honest response left is to face them. We cannot fault anyone for looking away before they’ve seen it, for sure. Yet once the awareness arrives, once one have separated it from the blur of the lifeworld, going back into comforting illusions feels like a cold self-betrayal. The Absurd - you cannot unsee it. Pretending otherwise, it is not peace, it is deception.

So we need to resist. But not by denying it exists, but by accepting it completely and refusing to surrender anyway. Revolt does not mean defeating the silence; rather it means refusing to let the silence squash, hollow and marginalise us out. We resist to stay lucid, true to ourselves.[^3] We resist to drag color into a black-and-white picture of reality We resist to insist on passion where none was promised.

Of course when judged by the usual criteria, this resistance looks futile. Some things do not exist to be won. Sometimes as everyday thinkers and tumblr stickers[^4] love to say -it’s the road that matters, not the destination. I agree to some extent, not because the journey is easy or romantic but because the struggle itself is the only thing that belongs to us and takes shape. The Absurd cannot be beaten but it can be fought actively, met head on. And pperhaps that sole idea is enough for us to keep going.
