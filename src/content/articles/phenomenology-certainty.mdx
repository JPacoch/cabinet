There’s a common mistake that shows up constantly in a modern discourse - we treat scientific knowledge as if it floats above experience. Many assume it even replaces the experience rather than emerges from it. The diagram below is meant to challenge that assumption - not by rejecting the science itself, but by placing it more precisely within a broader pipeline of quest for certainty.

At the foundation lies what phenomenologists call the Life-World - a raw, lived experience. This is the world as it is given - before theories, measurements or any interpretations. It’s messy but it’s also the only place anything ever actually shows up.

From there we perform phenomenological reduction. It's an attempt to suspend assumptions. But here's a shocker - interpretation doesn’t begin with science. It begins with commitment.

Before we do science or metaphysics, we specifically choose how we’re going to interpret reality. Science isn't born from nothing. It's built on a foundation to treat reality in a particular, constrained way. Science constantly narrows its focus. It strips away subjective, qualitative aspects of experience and replaces them with measurable, formalized structures.

Both paths (mataphysics and naturalism) merge into a local synthesis. This is a contextual integration of formal knowledge (from science) and lived meaning (from experience and metaphysical analysis). This synthesis is *local* because it depends on context, purpose, and domain. There is no single, final perspective.

So which perspective takes priority? Well, it depends on the context really. What is important is that metaphysics and science are not interchangeble. Naturalistic models can reshape our experience interpretations, but they cannot invalidate the fact of that experience itself.
